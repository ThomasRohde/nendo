# Designs, templates and brand kits

The **Design** tab gives a deck its look. A **design** from Brandkits is the whole look in one pick. A **template** sets the fonts, the colours and the shapes. A **brand kit** puts your organisation's colours, fonts and logo on top of the template.

## A design from Brandkits

A design carries everything the Design tab can choose: the template family, light or dark, the title weight, corners, kicker style and bullet, the colours, the fonts, the chart colours, the logo, the logo for dark slides, a small mark, where the logo sits, and the background of the title, section, closing and content slides, as a colour, a gradient or a picture.

1. In **Brandkits**, open the kit, set up its slides on the **Slides** tile, and choose **Export**, then **Deck design**. You get one `.deckdesign` file.
2. In Deck, on the **Design** tab, choose **Import from Brandkits…** and pick that file, or drop it on the tab.

The design shows under **Designs**, and the deck you are on starts using it at once. Its footer and slide numbers are used only where the deck has none. Pick another design card to change the look of a deck. Export the same kit again after a change in Brandkits and import it again: Deck updates the design in place, so every deck that uses it changes too.

A design is a template and a brand kit that belong together. You find both under **Templates** and **Brand kits**, and you can still pick another template with the design's kit.

## Six templates

| Template | Its look |
| --- | --- |
| **Keynote** | A dark stage, one bright accent, big type. For talks and launches. |
| **Editorial** | Warm paper, serif headlines and rules, like a long read. |
| **Swiss** | A white grid, heavy grotesque type and one red. |
| **Boardroom** | Navy and white with a quiet band. For reviews and board papers. |
| **Studio** | Soft gradients and rounded cards. For product and design stories. |
| **Minimal** | Hairlines, light type and room to breathe. |

Each template is a record. Open one from **Templates** to change its fonts or colours, or copy one to make your own: a copy keeps its family's shapes and takes your colours and fonts.

## A brand kit only, from Brandkits

A Nendo file cannot read another file, so a kit comes across as an export. Choose this when you want the kit's colours and logo on one of Deck's own templates.

1. In **Brandkits**, open the kit and choose **Export**. Pick the **Claude Design bundle (zip)**, the **design tokens (JSON)** or the **CSS variables**.
2. In Deck, on the **Design** tab, choose **Import from Brandkits…** and pick that file, or drop it on the tab.

Deck reads the kit's primary, secondary and accent colours, its background, surface, text and muted colours, its heading, body and mono fonts, its voice and tagline, and, from the bundle, its logo and its logo for dark backgrounds. The deck you are on starts using the kit at once. Import the same kit again, after a change in Brandkits, and Deck updates what the file holds and keeps the rest.

A kit's logo shows large on the title and closing slides and small at the top of most others, or where the design puts it, and as the mark when the design has one; on a dark slide Deck uses the logo for dark backgrounds. A logo picture on its own, dropped on the tab, goes onto the kit the deck uses.

## Colours stay legible

A brand's colours can clash with a template: dark text on a dark stage, or a pale accent on white. Deck checks each colour against the slide behind it and darkens or lightens it until it can be read. A dark template keeps its dark stage and a light template its light one, and the kit's colours become accents.

## Fonts

Deck draws with the fonts your computer has. A font from Google Fonts loads when you are online. PowerPoint uses the fonts of the computer that opens the file, so give the file to people who have the brand's fonts, or expect PowerPoint to substitute them.
