                      How the garden works

                      Write a note and link it to another with [[double brackets]]. Save, and the garden records who links where, plants the notes you named but have not written yet, and draws it all as a graph.

                      Start in four movesPlant, link, save, watch it grow
                      1. Plant a note. New note opens a blank page in Edit. Give it a title and a few lines.
                      2. Link it. Type [[ and pick a note from the list, or name one that does not exist yet: saving plants it as a Seed.
                      3. Save. Ctrl S turns links, tags and tasks into records in one step, and Undo takes the whole step back.
                      4. Watch it grow. The note's connections, the links in and out with a small graph of them, appear under it while you read, and the Graph shows the whole garden.

                      Keeping a journal? opens today's note, made from the note whose kind is Template.

                      What you write becomes recordsLinks, tags and tasks, from Markdown
                      WriteOn save it becomes
                      [[start-here]]
                      [[Start here]]                      		A Link to the note with that slug or title, with the sentence it sits in. That note lists this one in its backlinks.
                      [[start-here|the welcome]]The same link, showing your own words.
                      [[A new idea]]A link, and a new Seed note called A new idea, waiting to be written.
                      #readingA Tag, made once for the garden, and the note is filed under it.
                      - [ ] Call SamA Task on the garden's task list. Tick it while reading and the tick is saved at once.
                      `[[code]]`Nothing: inside code, brackets and hashes stay text.

                      The rest is everyday Markdown: # Heading, **bold**, *italic*, > quote, lists, links and fenced code. Links, tags and tasks somebody added by hand are marked Manual, and a save never touches them.

                      The life of a noteSeed, growing, evergreen
                      1. Seed A name and little else, often planted by a link. The front page counts the seeds that wait.
                      2. Growing Being written and linked. Most notes live here.
                      3. Evergreen Says what it means and is worth linking to. Mark it evergreen from the menu.

                      An unlinked note has no links in or out. Link it to something, and the graph will pull it in.

                      Find your wayThe tree, the dividers, previews and Find
                      • The tree is the outline. A note can sit under another. Click the arrow beside a note to fold its branch away, or use ← and →. Expand all and Collapse all are in the Note menu. Opening a note unfolds the branch it is in.
                      • Make room. Drag the line between the tree and the page to widen either. In Edit, drag the line between the Markdown and the preview; drag it to the right edge to fold the preview away. A double-click resets both.
                      • Read at your width. Narrow, Medium or Full, from the row above the page.
                      • Peek before you jump. Hover a link to preview its note. Follow it, and Nendo's Back returns you.
                      • Find a note by any word in it, title or body, with Find; the words are highlighted in the note you open, read or edited.

                      Working with an agentThe garden's own skill

                      This garden carries its own instructions for agents, the dev.nendo.garden skill. An agent connected to Nendo finds a note by its slug, reads its summary instead of the whole body, and asks the garden who links where instead of reading every note. How much an agent may change is yours to set, under Agent in Nendo.
