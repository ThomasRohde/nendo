# Find your way

As a garden grows, these keep it easy to walk through.

## The tree is the outline

Notes nest: a note can sit under another note, so the garden is one tree rather than folders of files. Click the arrow beside a note to fold its branch away, and again to unfold it. With the keyboard, `→` unfolds a branch or steps into it, and `←` folds it or steps up to the note above.

- **Show**, above the tree, folds it to a level: **1** shows only the top notes, **2** and **3** show that many levels, and **All** unfolds every branch. The level the tree shows is lit; fold a branch by hand and it goes out.
- Opening a note unfolds the branch it is in, so you always see where you are.
- What you fold is remembered on this device.

To move a note under another, or change its order, use the **Tree** screen and drag it into place.

## Make room

- Drag the line between the tree and the page to widen either. `←` and `→` move it when it has focus; a double-click puts it back.
- Set the page's width with **Narrow**, **Medium** or **Full** in the row above it.

## Read around a note

When a note has tasks, a grey strip under its title says how many are done and holds the next one to do, with its box. Tick it there or in the text: it is the same task. **Show all** lists every task of the note, with its due date and a way to its own page.

Under every note, while you read:

- **Local graph**, folded until you open it: this note and every note one link away, either way, across the width of the page, each dot in the colour of the section it grows in, named under the graph. Click a dot to go there. It stays open or folded as you left it.
- Any tags written on the note by hand. The tags in the body are the pills in the text itself.

Hover a link to preview the note it names without leaving the page. Follow it, and Nendo's **Back** brings you back.

## Find a note

**Find** in the row above the page searches what your notes say as you type: their titles, slugs and everything in their bodies. The notes it finds are in bold. Open one and the words you typed are highlighted in it, when you read it and when you edit it, and the page starts at the first of them. The tree keeps the branches that hold a match, open or folded.

Every word you type must be in the note, though not in the same place, so `compost worms` finds a note titled *Compost* that mentions worms further down. Put words in double quotes to find them together, and put a minus before a word to leave out the notes that contain it. The last word also finds longer words that start with it, so you can stop typing early.

Find uses the garden's search index, which Nendo keeps current every time you save. If the file has no index yet, Find still looks through every note's title, slug and body. Nendo can build the index from Studio or Ctrl K.
